# Sales voice

**Owner's rule (2026-11-06): anything customer facing takes this voice.**
That means pitches, call scripts, outreach and follow-up emails, proposals
and term sheets, marketing pages, demo walk-throughs, and anything written to
a prospect, partner or agency.

Text inside the app keeps DESIGN.md "Voice". The two agree: confident and
direct, never salesy, nothing invented.

The base is Matt Easton's consultative selling ([Easton University](https://www.eastonuniversity.com/)).
Alex Hormozi's principles come through it, softened: never his bold, hard-close
execution. The "scorecard" and "7 steps" below are the owner's coaching
synthesis of Easton's themes, not an official Easton rubric.

## In one sentence
Ask before telling, find the outcome the customer is after, and always leave
with a dated next step.

## Philosophy
1. **Ditch the pitch.** Pressure builds the wall you then have to climb. Pitching
   without uncovered value leaves discounting as the only lever.
2. **Sell the outcome, not the product.** Find why this person is talking to you,
   and what result they want in their business.
3. **Help them decide for their own reasons.** Understand, listen, then let them
   persuade themselves.
4. **Selling is a learned skill.** Run the process consistently and get a little
   better each time.

## Principles
1. **Lead with questions, not statements.** No yes-ladders or engineered yeses.
2. **Slow down.** Discover the value before presenting anything.
3. **Find the pain and the cost of doing nothing.** People act because of pain.
4. **Think like a doctor.** Diagnose before prescribing, and say the hard thing
   when it is true.
5. **Let peers do the convincing.** "Others in your situation came to us
   because..." Only with real stories (see Honesty).
6. **Most objections aren't objections.** Ask for their ideas first, and take
   "I need to think about it" at face value.
7. **Be next-step obsessed.** Every conversation ends with a next step that has a
   date and an owner. Summarize it before you leave. Close on progress.

## The 7 steps (when a customer describes a problem)
1. **Understand:** "Tell me about that."
2. **Expand:** "How often does that happen?"
3. **Measure:** "What does that cost?" Ask for the ingredients (how many, how
   long, how often), not a total nobody can answer.
4. **Humanize:** "Who gets dragged into that?"
5. **Vision:** "What happens if that goes away?"
6. **Story:** a relevant, true example.
7. **Offer:** "Would it be helpful if I showed you?"

The weakness to work on: finding pain is solid; **measuring** it is the step
that moves from good salesperson to trusted advisor.

## Lines that work
| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "What brought you in today?" | "What's the most important thing we need to accomplish today?" |
| "Are you on a time crunch?" | "How much time do I have with you?" |
| "We're the best at X." | "Others in your situation came to us because..." |
| "Do you want to buy?" (yes/no) | "Does it make sense to move forward?" |
| (after a no) silence or "OK, thanks" | "Okay. What's a good next step?" |
| (after "I don't know") | "What's a good next step for me, then?" |
| "Call me if you need anything." | "Let's meet Thursday the 14th to go over it; I'll bring X, you'll pull Y." |
| A statement after hearing a number | A question: "Wait, how often?" "What's behind that number?" |

## Hormozi, through this voice
- **Value equation** (dream outcome × likelihood ÷ time × effort) becomes
  *questions* about the outcome, the cost of waiting and the effort today, not
  claims.
- **Reason why:** one plain sentence on why you're reaching out.
- **Lower the risk of saying yes:** small, specific asks ("20 minutes, at your
  office").
- **Not his execution:** no bold guarantees, no urgency tricks, no scarcity,
  no hard closes.

## How a pitch is judged (scorecard)
| Category | Weight | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity | 10% | They feel you want to understand their world |
| Discovery depth | 15% | Root cause, not the symptom |
| Pain identified | 15% | Real business problems, not generic gripes |
| Pain expanded | 15% | They realize "yes, that really is a problem" |
| Financial impact | 20% | The problem in their own numbers |
| Story relevance | 10% | A true example close to their situation |
| Trust | 5% | They share more, ask your advice |
| Who decides | 5% | You know who owns, approves and evaluates |
| Commitment | pass/fail | A dated next step with an owner. Without one, the call failed, whatever else scored. |

Grades: A+ trusted advisor (they discuss budgets, politics, priorities and ask
for advice), A consultative, B competent but too much solution talk, C vendor,
D catalog reader, F product dump.

## Honesty (non-negotiable)
- **Never invent a story, customer, number or result.** YardCard Elite has no
  customer stories yet. Honest substitutes:
  - the owner's own live booking and refund;
  - the owner's own yard card rentals, only if real;
  - the owner's industrial-sales experience.
- **State status plainly:** no paying agencies yet; a founding-partner offer
  instead.
- Same rule as DESIGN.md: no fake testimonials, logos or stats.

## Practice habits
- Three questions deep before mentioning the company.
- Tripwires: hear a number, ask a question; hear pain described as normal, ask
  "why?"
- Write every number down and come back to each one.
- Write the next step's date in the notes before standing up.
